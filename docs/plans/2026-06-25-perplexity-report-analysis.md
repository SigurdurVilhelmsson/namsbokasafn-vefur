# Analysis — Perplexity "Digital Reading Platform Report" vs. our reader plan

**Date:** 2026-06-25
**Reviewed against:** `docs/plans/2026-04-22-screen-vs-paper-reader-plan.md` (the P0–P3 reader plan), `docs/plans/2026-06-10-audit-remediation-and-reader-v1.1-roadmap.md` (branch status), `CLAUDE.md`.
**Report under review:** Perplexity-generated "Digital Reading Platform Report for OpenStax Icelandic Textbooks."

## TL;DR

The report is a competent generic playbook, but it is **strictly weaker than the plan we already have** on the one axis that matters most — the structural drivers of the screen-inferiority effect — and it **omits our single highest-evidence intervention entirely** (anti-scroll pagination, Salmerón et al. 2024). Its real value is as a **completeness checklist**: it surfaces three areas our pipeline under-weights — a _systematic accessibility workstream_, _glossary "terms-learned" tracking + export_, and _checkpoint feedback that explains "why"_. Those are the actionable output.

Treat the report as a backlog input, not a competing design.

## Reliability caveat (read first)

The report's citation apparatus is **broken on its face** — you can see it without chasing a single link:

- The inline tokens (`[web:10]`, `[web:12]`, `[web:54]`…) **do not map to the numbered footnote list**. Footnote `[^10]`, for example, is a Harvard WCAG vendor-checklist `.xlsx`, not "Clinton 2019" as the prose implies. So none of the specific empirical claims are traceable to a source you can open.
- Its evidence base is thin: **Clinton 2019** (one meta-analysis) plus several **OpenStax accessibility marketing/help pages**. It does **not** cite **Delgado et al. 2018** or **Salmerón et al. 2024** — the two papers our plan's structural interventions are built on.

Net: trust the report's _direction_ (it aligns with mainstream UX guidance), distrust any _specific number or effect claim_ it states. Our plan cites primary literature with effect sizes; the report does not.

## What's actually worth adding (proposed backlog)

The three genuine gaps, sized in the plan's "Claude Code session" unit and slotted against existing tiers:

1. **Accessibility workstream (highest priority).** Promote a11y from a side-effect of P0.4 to a named track: a WCAG-2.1-AA pass (keyboard, focus order, contrast already mostly there), alt-text + long-description coverage audit for figures (this is **efni** content work — coordinate cross-repo), and a screen-reader reading-order check over headings/notes/examples/checkpoints. _Suggest: a new "P2.5 — accessibility audit" or a standalone track; ~2–3 sessions vefur-side + a content audit in efni._ Note the **MathML question** here: decide deliberately whether pre-rendered MathJax SVG carries adequate accessible markup, or whether efni should emit MathML — that's a pipeline decision, not a reader tweak.

2. **Glossary "terms-learned" + export (medium).** A chapter-scoped tracker of terms encountered/starred, surfaced in `/ordabok`, with the same export affordance annotations already have. _Fits naturally beside P2.3 (recall-review tab); ~1–1.5 sessions._

3. **Explanatory checkpoint feedback (medium).** Quiz/checkpoint feedback that states _why_ the answer is right and links back to the source sentence/figure (we already have `answerLinks.ts` bidirectional nav to build on). _Slots into the Phase-2 quiz-bank work (audit M2); ~1.5 sessions._

Everything else in the report is either already shipped, already planned at greater depth, or a recommendation we've consciously out-designed (the scrolling reading view).

## One-line verdict

The report is a useful **external completeness check** that independently re-derives most of our v1.1/v1.2 decisions from weaker evidence — and in doing so highlights the three things we _haven't_ systematized: accessibility, glossary learning-tracking, and explanatory feedback. It does **not** challenge any decision we've made; where it diverges (scrolling reading view, MathML), our plan is better-grounded.
