## 1. Executive summary

The review judged v1.1 (`dde768e`, reader plan P0) and v1.2 (`e3b29c7`, plan P1) against main (`3dbf50b`). Of its 110 issues, 7 are critical, 6 of them in the user interface. The seventh, inconsistent key terms in the preview content's machine translation (in §1.5 'nákvæmni' means accuracy, then precision), is efni's to fix and confirms the sync hold [PED-05]. Content numbers come from efni's ② preview, not the live site; no real device or screen reader was used. No study tests 16–20-year-olds reading chemistry, or any STEM text, on phones.

### Verdict

- **v1.1** has the right interventions on a sound pager [KEEP-03], but accessibility blockers and a silent switch of every existing profile to paged keep it from students [PED-01].
- **v1.2**'s June P0 is superseded, its pager broken on today's efni markup, so no P0 file comes from it [CODE-04]; its four P1 features need rework before they ship.

### Key findings

#### Pedagogy

- Most interventions take their weakest form: recall without comparison, a rating that counts a correct but effortful recall ('Erfitt') as a lapse, and a pre-question posing an objective, not a question [PED-07, PED-08, PED-19].
- Paging is a hypothesis (no significant paging/scrolling difference in a full-text meta-analysis, phones excluded); in the preview content it strands worked examples and parts figures from their text [PED-01, PED-02, PED-03].
- The recall prompt can fire on 217 pages, 103 of them not sections (preview content) [PED-04]; on main, 'Æfa' reviews every card, due or not, and practice questions lose text or exponents [PED-11, PED-12, PED-13].

#### Code

- The settings store saves every default on first load, freezing the first one shipped into every profile; it must store only readers' choices before any default changes [CODE-05].
- Two known breaks git cannot see: main's flashcard test expects tap-to-flip, and the recall store lacks main's rename hook [CODE-01, CODE-02].
- Pager edge cases fail (keys, swipes, print, links), and 25 of 40 planted bugs survive v1.1's unit tests [CODE-06 to CODE-10, CODE-20].
- v1.2's P1 rules for calibration, the pre-question gate and pick, and the cloze (fill-in-the-blank) text step sit inline in components that no test loads, and 14 of its 21 planted bugs survive. With the Icelandic term alone selected, at most 132 of 848 cloze fronts come out clean in the preview content [CODE-14, CODE-11].

#### User interface

- White text on amber (2.92:1 in light mode) and the light focus ring fail WCAG AA contrast site-wide [UI-02, UI-07].
- Focus is lost after predictions and recall saves, neither is announced, and screen readers miss which Settings button is selected and the visible sidebar [UI-01, UI-03, UI-06].
- Paged pages put the section-finishing 'Merkja kafla sem lesinn' under 'Næsta', fill at most 53–67% of the screen and, after arrival, lose the machine-translation notice [UI-04, UI-08, UI-09].
- Ported as written onto v1.1's pager, v1.2's pre-question card would hide its button under the sticky header on 6 of 9 measured paged arrivals (computed). 'Eyðukort' is likely out of reach on phones and keyboards, because the selection popup (on main too) opens only on a mouse release. Kvörðun's matrix has no row or column headers [CODE-13, UI-13, UI-18].

### Way forward

Recommendation for [USER]: strategy C, smallest shippable increments. Freeze v1.1 as the porting source; move each piece to main as a small PR, shipped on code-only trains (one deploy carrying several PRs): accessibility fixes, live-site corrections, predict-first, recall, then a choices-only settings store. The pager lands dark (live, but reachable only by special links), so nobody's mode changes until [USER] rules; the narrow measure (shorter default lines) and P1 follow, one feature at a time.

Section 7 lists nine things to keep. Restoring tap-to-flip, building pages by moving blocks or dropping a prompt to cut friction would undo deliberate work.

### Four open calls for [USER]

The three rulings stand.

1. **Page height.** Today's 53–67%, a fixed share, equal lines per device, or maximise usable lines. Window height showed no comprehension effect (adults, full text). Recommendation for [USER]: maximise usable lines, ruled after reading paged chapters.
2. **Recall unit.** Per section, per sub-section with guards, or per section plus a capped interim prompt; repeated identical prompts lost effect (full text). Recommendation for [USER]: per section, made stronger, not more frequent.
3. **Launch default.** Scrolled, paged, paged for new profiles, or a rule on screen height. Defaults stick (abstract). Recommendation for [USER]: decide after a classroom crossover (each student reads both ways), by a rule fixed beforehand: paged only if not worse within a preset margin, otherwise scrolled.
4. **Pre-question frequency.** Every unread visit (as built [PED-06]), first visit per section, a first visit capped per session or day, or the first section per chapter. The benefit covers only what was asked, in teenagers too (full text). Recommendation for [USER]: less often than every unread visit, starting with the capped first visit, reviewed after 4–6 weeks of local counters; decide before P1.2 ships (live pages already carry objectives).

## 4. (a) Pedagogy: what the research supports

### 4.1 In short

- The retrieval interventions are the right ones, but most are built in their weakest form: unbounded recall with no comparison, an ease rating that counts effortful successes as failures, and an objective to "think about" instead of a question [PED-07, PED-08, PED-19].
- Paging (P0.4) is a hypothesis, not "the highest-evidence intervention"; in the preview content it strands worked examples and parts figures from their text [PED-01, PED-02, PED-03].
- Before the recall prompt reaches students, gate it by page type (103 of its 217 sites are not sections) [PED-04]; and address three defects live on main: 'Æfa' reviewing every card each time, due or not (a call for [USER] on the SRS, the spaced-repetition scheduler), and two that break practice items [PED-11, PED-12, PED-13].
- Redesign P1 before the port: a real pre-question, with its frequency (open call 4) settled first [PED-06, PED-07], and Kvörðun led by an action [PED-09]; verbatim cloze is the least supported card type [PED-23].
- Nothing records a prompt shown, skipped or abandoned, or a mode switch (saved recalls and predictions are already stored); on-device counters and a spring-term crossover would supply data for open calls 2–4 [PED-17, PED-35].
- Term drift in the preview content confirms the sync hold; it is efni's to fix [PED-05].

### 4.2 What the research can and cannot say

Six research strands inform this section (R1 reading surface, R2 retrieval and calibration, R3 STEM, R4 typography and language, R5 prompting, R6 platforms). Three limits apply.

- **No study tests this audience.** None tests 16–20-year-olds reading chemistry, or any STEM text, on phones. The evidence comes from university students, adults or younger pupils, mostly in single sessions, never with machine-translated text. The closest are high-school classrooms (Yang 2021), 16-year-olds (Stenlund 2016) and teenagers (King-Shepard 2025).
- **Much was read as abstracts.** Numbers carry their level: (ft) full text, (abs) abstract, (sec) secondary source. Effect sizes (g, d) are group differences in standard deviations; n.s. means not significant.
- **Content numbers come from efni's ② preview**, not the live site.

Digital textbooks beat paper slightly overall (g = 0.22), but no advantage was found beyond 24 weeks, in individualist-culture samples or on course grades and final exams (Lei & Yang 2024, abs; mostly collectivist-culture samples, 4 high-school effect sizes). Adolescents' best-documented screen deficit is overconfidence, seen even with the whole text on one screen (Ronconi 2022, abs). Both point to retrieval, feedback and calibration more than to layout.

Research on e-books in schools adds context, not tests of reader features. In the one upper-secondary e-book log study found, 80 Japanese 10th graders in a maths course barely used search, bookmarks or embedded quizzes, and students whose scores fell kept adding and deleting highlights (Geng 2024, ft; correlational, one school, 5 students in the improved group). Among 15-year-olds, up to an hour a day of device use for learning at school went with higher maths scores, and distraction by classmates' devices with 15 points lower (OECD 2023, PISA 2022, ft; associations only). In the closest population match, secondary chemistry, an open textbook did at least as well as a commercial one (Robinson 2014, abs). Neither Sweden's return to printed textbooks nor Norway's advice to read more on paper rested on outcome studies: Sweden acted on a reading-score decline and precaution (Edholm 2022, ft), Norway on reviews of mostly university studies (Skjermbrukutvalget 2023, ft). Korea's 2025 downgrade of AI textbooks to optional material cited teacher resistance, screen time, literacy and budget, with no learning-outcome data reported (Kwon 2025, ft). The findings fit three choices in this section: look-backs as cheap as going forward [KEEP-04], highlighting presented as a convenience, not as studying [PED-31], and a one-tap error report on machine-translated pages [PED-15].

The plan rests on a Claude-generated literature review (April 2026) that is not in the repository, and several attributions are wrong or overstated (Appendix B). CLAUDE.md's design principle overstates too: the testing-effect literature backs answered retrieval, not an objective-statement pre-question or pagination. This section keeps the interventions and makes them skippable, faded and measured; changing how they are delivered is in scope, removing one is not. Rewording CLAUDE.md is [USER]'s edit [KEEP-01].

Icelandic strings marked (proposed) are not in the code; each needs a native-speaker check (D20, 8.5).

### 4.3 P0.1 Narrow measure (v1.1)

**What it does.** 'Þröngt' (38rem) becomes the default: 68.2 characters per line on a desktop at 16 px, while phones stay at 36–37 whatever the setting [UI-31].

**Evidence (R1, R4).** The 50–75-character band is convention and comfort, not comprehension evidence; the NN/g source the plan cites was not found (Appendix B). Long lines read faster; one adult study favoured 55 over 100 characters for comprehension (Dyson 2004 review, ft). Short lines helped high-school students with dyslexia (Schneps 2013, ft).

**What it gets right.** A moderate desktop default; phones left alone.

**What to change.** Set the widths in em so the measure follows the font size (today 81 characters at 14 px, 56 at 20 px), and reword the code comment [PED-33]. Add a help line under 'Línubreidd' and pin the default in a test [UI-31]. It reaches new profiles only [CODE-05].

**Open call.** None.

### 4.4 P0.2 Recall prompt (v1.1)

**What it does.** On a section's first completion, a typed recall box, 'Kafli lesinn — rifjaðu upp', replaces main's 'Vel gert!' and asks for 'það helsta sem þú manst úr kaflanum'. 'Sleppa' closes it; a saved entry is never shown again.

**Evidence (R2, R3, R5).**

- Classroom quizzing works: g = 0.499 overall, 0.655 in high school, 0.340 in chemistry on 10 effects (Yang 2021, ft). Free recall was the weakest format (0.238), short answer among the strongest (0.638), and corrective feedback raised effects (0.537 against 0.374), all between-study comparisons.
- Recall pays off after a delay (Roediger & Karpicke 2006, abs), and delayed, not immediate, summaries improved students' judgement of what they know (Yang 2023, ft).
- Self-scores need a standard: middle-school students credited 73% of their wrong definitions without one and 44% with the definition in view (Lipko 2009, abs).
- For MT text: after low-cohesion text, generative prompts beat retrieval (Roelle & Nückles 2019, abs); this MT's cohesion is untested.

**What it gets right.** Typed recall (overt beat covert retrieval for key-term definitions; Tauber 2018, abs) after the content, one tap to skip, nothing gated, and an informational acknowledgement rather than a completion reward, which lowers motivation (Deci 1999, abs) [KEEP-05].

**What to change.**

1. Gate by page type. It fires on rollups (a chapter's summary, key-term, exercise and key-equation pages), introductions and the preface: 103 of 217 sites in the preview content. The cause is the missing gate, not the ruling that rollups always scroll [PED-04].
2. Name the section and bound the request, e.g. 'Hver eru 2–3 meginatriði kaflans?' (proposed); question wording alone made no significant difference (Bisra 2018, ft). Hide the Ctrl+Enter hint on phones [PED-18].
3. After saving, let the student tick which sentences of the section's summary they covered; label that machine-translated list 'Vélþýtt' and gate it on efni's terminology pass [PED-19, PED-05].
4. Keep unsent drafts, offer 'Rifja upp aftur' (proposed) [PED-19], and add the recall store's rename hook in the change that brings it to main (S5.1) [CODE-02].

**Open call 2 (per section or per sub-section).** The plan asks for per sub-section; v1.1 prompts per section [PED-16]. In the preview content the 114 numbered sections hold 428 paged units, and 172 of them already hold a Check-your-learning question. Interim recall helped later sections in the lab (Wissman 2011, abs), but identical prompts repeated over a term lost their effect (Nückles 2010, ft, university students), and scrolled readers have no unit boundaries. Section 8.2 gives the options and the recommendation.

### 4.5 P0.3 Predict-first ratings (v1.1)

**What it does.** The flashcard front asks 'Man það' or 'Man það ekki' before the answer shows; both reveal it, then the card is rated. The prediction is stored but never reaches SM-2, the spaced-repetition scheduler [KEEP-02].

**Evidence (R2).** The plan's sources show screen readers are somewhat worse calibrated (Clinton 2019, abs: g = 0.20) but test no prediction before an answer (Appendix B). In research on judgements of learning (forecasts of remembering), judgements made later, from the question alone, are far more accurate (Rhodes & Tauber 2011, abs: g = 0.93), yet making one barely changes memory (Double 2018, abs: g = 0.054). So predict-first is a monitoring device; any learning comes from a retrieval attempt, which the two buttons invite but do not ensure [PED-20]. Dropping 'known' cards hurt learning, and confident errors are the likeliest to be corrected (Kornell & Bjork 2008; Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001; both abs).

**What it gets right.** A judgement from the question alone, no card dropped, scheduling untouched [KEEP-02]. The change that brings it to main (S4.1) must adapt main's flashcard tests to it, not restore tap-to-flip [CODE-01].

**What to change.**

- The rating asks 'Hversu auðvelt var þetta?' and counts 'Erfitt' as a lapse: a card on a 6-day interval drops to 1 day, and a ported Kvörðun would call an effortful success wrong. [USER] chooses between relabelling by correctness, scoring 'Erfitt' as a pass, or asking correctness first, after seeing the interval table; it is pre-existing on main [PED-08].
- 'Æfa' reviews every card each time, so four same-day 'Gott' ratings schedule a card 38 days ahead, though spaced practice beats massed (Latimier 2021, ft: g = 0.74 after correction for publication bias). Make 'Æfa' a due session; this is live on main and [USER]'s SRS call [PED-11].
- Echo the prediction (proposed: 'Þú sagðir: Man það'), bring a confident error back once at the session's end [PED-20], and build the card once for /minniskort and /nam [CODE-23].

**Open call.** None.

### 4.8 P1.2 Pre-question (v1.2, to port)

**What it does.** Before an unread section, a card headed 'Áður en þú byrjar' shows one automatically picked objective under 'Hugleiddu í smástund …', with one button, 'Ég hugleiddi þetta — byrja að lesa'. **Evidence (R2, R3, R5).** The effect is real but narrow: g = 0.54 on the asked item and 0.04 on the rest (St. Hilaire 2024, abs); for teenagers 0.51 and 0.08 (King-Shepard 2025, ft). ### 4.9 P1.3 One-tap cloze (v1.2, to port)

**What it does.** 'Eyðukort' in the selection popup blanks the selected words in their sentence (a cloze, or fill-in-the-blank, card) and saves it silently to a deck of that name.

**Evidence (R2, R4, R6).** In classroom studies, fill-in-the-blank quizzes had the largest effect of the quiz formats compared (Yang 2021, ft: g = 0.773, k = 22, a between-study moderator). The case against verbatim cloze rests on transfer: fill-in-the-blank practice helped only the exact items (Hinze & Wiley 2011, abs), and higher-order and mixed quizzes, not fact quizzes, improved higher-order performance (Agarwal 2019, abs). Self-made cards beat premade ones (Pan 2023, sec). School students gained little from their own highlighting (Ponce 2022, abs: 0.24, against 0.39 for college). No experiment testing highlight-to-card conversion was found (Appendix B), and a verbatim card from MT text rehearses the MT phrasing (reasoning, not a result).

**What it gets right.** The button sits where students already act, and the builder is small and tested.

**What to change.**

- With the Icelandic term alone selected, at most 132 of 848 key-term fronts in the preview content are clean and 534 end in a dangling '(e.'; selecting the whole bold term gives 75.5–82% clean. Fix the text step whatever the design [CODE-11].

- [USER] picks the design (D07, 8.5) [PED-23]: (a) the verbatim cloze as specified, with its text step fixed; (b) the middle path: a tap on a key term makes a term card whose back the student writes before the definition (labelled 'Vélþýtt') shows, other selections get a template, and editable key-term decks are added; (c) no separate button: the existing 'Minniskort' dialog opens with the front prefilled and the back for the student to write; (d) defer it. The reviewers ranked the verbatim cloze lowest and saw (c) as the smaller change. Recommendation for [USER]: (b).
- Present highlighting as a convenience, not as studying, with one route into a card [PED-31].

### 4.10 P1.4 Fonts and theme (v1.2, to port)

**What it does.** Adds Atkinson Hyperlegible (the original 1.006, not the 'Next' family the plan names), relabels OpenDyslexic 'Sumir lesendur kjósa þetta letur', labels Atkinson 'Hannað fyrir hámarks læsileika', and follows the system theme when none is stored.

### 4.11 Cross-cutting matters

**Term consistency.** In the preview content, §1.5, which teaches accuracy against precision, uses 'nákvæmni' for accuracy in its definition and for precision in its worked example; 94 of 465 in-text term forms differ from the glossary's; and the Check-your-learning heading has 16 wordings [PED-05, critical]. **MT notice.** In a small adult pilot, trust fell with disfluent MT far more than with fluent but inaccurate MT (Martindale & Carpuat 2018, abs), so fluent errors may pass unnoticed. Give the notice a 'Tilkynna villu' (proposed) link to a prefilled /feedback [PED-15].

[USER] decides whether that event goes, becomes opt-in or is disclosed; the data rule and any school data sheet follow [KEEP-01, PED-39].

**Teacher fit.** Assigned reading was about three times higher where university instructors built it into the course (Russell 2023, abs), yet /for-teachers says nothing about the learning design [PED-39]. Add a teaching section and a first-encounter explainer per intervention [PED-25]; once [USER] has ruled calls 2 and 4, add two or three settings choices per intervention, all on by default, whose options and preselected choice follow those rulings (D17, 8.5) [PED-24]; fix /prof's claim that questions adapt after each answer [PED-29]. ### 6.3 Interaction and accessibility

#### Focus and announcements [UI-01]: critical, M

When a new component removes the focused control, focus falls to `<body>`: after predictions, ratings, key or swipe turns from a glossary term, recall saves or skips, and the pre-question. 'Vistað — góð upprifjun festir efnið í minni.' is in no live region (an area a screen reader reads out when it changes), which fails WCAG 4.1.3. Tab order survives; no screen reader was run. Before release: one shared `announce()` live region; the recall textarea named by its heading, and focus moved on after a save; after a prediction, focus on the revealed answer, never on the 'Aftur' rating button, where a held-down Enter key would rate the card.

#### Selected state [UI-03]: critical, S

'Línubil', 'Línubreidd' and 'Lestrarstilling' show the selected option only as an amber fill. They lack `aria-pressed`, which tells a screen reader a toggle is on, and a labelled group: a WCAG 4.1.2 Level A failure, live on main for the first two. The help text sends screen-reader users there ('Samfellt skrun hentar betur með skjálesurum'). Add a labelled `role="group"`, `aria-pressed` and a check icon on the selected segment, a fieldset and legend for 'Leturgerð', and a help line without the false 'án skruns'. The lasting fix is a shared segmented control (6.6): a row of options, one of them on.

#### Screen readers in paged mode [UI-05]: high, M

Paging hides off-page blocks completely. From page 2 there is no h1, and a say-all (a screen reader's continuous reading) skips the rest of the section (inferred from the code). The way to continuous reading is the 15th of 18 Tab stops in Settings. Keep the h1 for screen readers on every page, name the sub-section in the live text, add a 'Samfellt skrun' toggle to the toolbar and the pager bar, and focus the new page after a key or swipe turn. The fixes hold under any answer to open call 3, the launch default: scrolled for all, paged for all, paged for new profiles only, or a rule keyed on viewport height or aspect. If [USER] chooses a paged default, it should ship only once these fixes are in. Section 8.3 gives the evidence and the recommendation.

#### Keyboard and swipe [CODE-09]: high, S

The swipe guard is set out in 5.2 [CODE-09]. Space on a focused glossary term scrolls instead of turning the page, so let the term handle Space [UI-01].

#### Reduced motion [UI-25]: medium, S

Only two files honour prefers-reduced-motion, the system setting for less animation, so the recall prompt still flies in. On main, add a global reduced-motion rule, a `transitions.ts` wrapper that zeroes Svelte transitions, and a lint rule to keep it that way.

#### The sidebar's Tab order [UI-06]: critical, S

The closed sidebar, the default, is `aria-hidden`, yet from 1024 px CSS always shows it: the visible table of contents is hidden from screen readers while its links stay focusable. Below 1024 px its off-screen links stay in the Tab order, so on the phone 'Næsta' is 60 Tab presses from the top. On main, hide the closed sidebar below 1024 px with `visibility: hidden` and drop `aria-hidden`; aim for 'Næsta' within 20 presses.

## 7. What to keep

Nine register entries are things to keep. They work now, and a fix elsewhere could break them by accident. Protecting them takes mostly tests and review rules, not new features.

### Protecting the learner: the low-stakes stance

- **[KEEP-01] Prompts that never block, and no rewards for finishing.** Every prompt closes in one tap and gates nothing; both predict buttons reveal the answer; nothing awards badges, unlocks or ranks; no view compares a student with peers. Why: non-skippable prompts limited low performers' transfer (Pape & Thomas 2025, 72 German 6th graders, full text). The learning stores stay on the device, but a production build sends a page-view event on every route change inside a book; the data rule waits on [USER]'s choice to remove that page-view event, make it opt-in, or keep and disclose it.
- **[KEEP-02] Predict-first's shape.** 'Man það' / 'Man það ekki' on the card front, both revealing the answer, and `predictedKnown` kept out of SM-2 (the spaced-repetition scheduler). Why: on a due card this is a delayed judgment, more accurate than an immediate one (Rhodes & Tauber 2011, g = 0.93, abstract, mostly adults).
- **[KEEP-05] The recall prompt's form.** Typed recall after the content, 'Sleppa' in one tap, and 'Vistað — góð upprifjun festir efnið í minni.' instead of a celebration. Why: overt retrieval beats covert (Tauber et al. 2018, pooled d = 0.43, abstract). Keep this form whichever unit [USER] chooses in open call 2.
- **[KEEP-08] Worked solutions stay visible.** Only the Check-your-learning answer is hidden (main's class-only selection, which v1.1 lacks; every piece ported to main lands on top of it), examples precede problems as in OpenStax, answers pair with exercises by data attributes, and no example is split across pages. Why: worked examples beat problem-solving or business-as-usual controls in mathematics (Barbieri 2023, g = 0.48, full text).

### Protecting reading: the pager

- **[KEEP-03] Its architecture.** Pages are made by hiding blocks, never by moving them, with a pure `paginate()` and a `{#key}` remount per section. Why: highlights, the English gloss span and every content action keep working (no e2e test checks this yet). Change only the hiding mechanism, so print shows the whole section [CODE-08], and fix the failure path [CODE-17].
- **[KEEP-04] Its reader-facing design.** Whole-block breaks, sub-section units under 'Hluti N af M · Síða X af Y', one-tap look-backs, both modes, scrolling rollups (ruling 2), no imposed timer, deep links that open the right page. Why: split sentences increased rereading (Dillon 1990, adults, abstract). Hidden pages leave the accessibility tree (what screen readers read), so pair this with a continuous-reading switch, whichever default [USER] chooses.

### Protecting future work: the scaffolding

- **[KEEP-06] Rules in pure, tested modules.** `paginate()`, `buildSessionPlan`, main's `sectionRenames` and the cloze builder keep their rules out of the components. Why: one developer can change a rule safely only where a unit test pins it, so the rules the port and P2 add belong in such modules (`paginate.ts` itself still has 7 surviving mutants).

### Protecting new screens: the design system

- **[KEEP-07] Tokens, registry icons and phone-sized targets.** The pager's token-only colours, registry icons, 44 px phone targets, the pager's live region and the Settings dialog's focus handling. Why: the contrast and focus fixes should build on these. Not on the list: white text on amber and the light-theme focus ring, which fail WCAG.
- **[KEEP-09] Self-hosted Atkinson Hyperlegible and an explicit theme choice.** P1.4 drops OpenDyslexic's label 'Letur hannað fyrir lesblinda', and an explicit theme choice wins over the OS. Why: the four Atkinson faces cover all 20 Icelandic letters and „ “ with no outside request. Keep the theme rule, not v1.2's code, which freezes the OS theme on a first visit [UI-22].

These are the parts not to "simplify" while fixing the rest. Restoring tap-to-flip, building pages by moving blocks, folding a tested rule back into a component, or dropping a prompt to cut friction would pass as cleanup and undo deliberate work. Changing how an intervention is delivered is in scope; removing one is not.

## 8. Decisions for [USER]

The three rulings are settled: (1) on arrival the reader scrolls past the learning objectives, (2) chapter rollups always scroll, and (3) 'Næsta' on the last page finishes the section, even when sub-sections were skipped.

The four open calls stay open. No study tests 16–20-year-olds reading chemistry on phones. Section 10 uses these ids.

### 8.1 Call 1: the page-height budget (D-CALL1)

How much of the screen may a page fill, and so how many lines does it hold?

- (a) Maximise usable lines. Step 1, code only, removes the artefacts (about 67–68% of the screen on phone and desktop, 78% on tablet). Step 2 drops the 76 px clearance (about 77%), but only after the floating chrome is docked, or it covers 'Næsta' [UI-12]. Cost: inside S9.5.
- (b) A fixed share, such as 60%, after step 1. Cost: a constant.
- (c) A similar line count on every device. Cost: a per-device rule.
- (d) Today's 53–67%: in the preview content, 9–14 lines a page and 34 pages for §1.2 on phone and desktop. Cost: none.

**Evidence.** Window height showed no comprehension effect, though small windows cost page handling (R1-17, a review, full text; R1-18, abstract; adults at desktops). About 80–103 px of each page is code artefact [UI-08]. In the preview content, block breaks cap the average fill near 58–59% of the screen, and 100 px more joins only 3 of 18 split phone figure pairs [UI-08, PED-03].

**Measure.** An e2e step logs lines per page and window-scroll pages per viewport; [USER] reads a few chapters paged on phone and laptop.

**Recommendation for [USER]:** (a), step 1 first, judged by lines per page rather than tuned for learning; figures go through the lightbox. Rule after reading on the dark pager (S9.6).

**Decide before** S10.1, or before S9.6 under the early opt-in (D06). S9.5's last chunk sets the value; a ruling made after S9.6 ships as a small deploy before the lessons.

### 8.2 Call 2: recall per section or per sub-section (D-CALL2)

Does the recall prompt come once per section, or after each sub-section?

- (a) Per section, as built; a separate type gate keeps it off rollups, introductions and the Formáli [PED-04]. Cost: an erratum to plan line 189 and criterion 6.
- (b) Per sub-section, guarded: units of 400+ words, at most 2 per section, none after the last, footnote or title-only unit, faded after 3 saves or skips; about 125 units (preview content); paged only. Cost: +2–3 sessions, +1–2 for scrolled mode.
- (c) As (a), plus (b)'s capped interim prompt, piloted with one class before anyone else gets it, and only after the crossover, so it cannot bend that result. The pilot class switches it on through a one-time link that sets a flag on each device (S11.7). Cost: (b)'s prompt, paged only, plus that switch.
- An authored check question per sub-section is a low-severity extra needing efni questions (H10) [PED-16].

**Evidence.** For more prompts: interim tests helped recall of later sections (R2-03, abstract, lab). For a stronger prompt: identical weekly prompts lost their effect while faded ones kept it (R5-05, full text, one university study), though fading did not change STEM scaffolding effects (R5-02, abstract); free recall was the weakest classroom quiz format, feedback raised effects, and a standard to compare against made students' self-scores more accurate (R2-01, full text; R2-18, abstract; numbers in 4.4).

**Measure.** Local counters (shown, saved, skipped, abandoned), read from /greining in class or handed in as a counts-only export. They show uptake, not efficacy.

**Recommendation for [USER]:** (a), made stronger rather than more frequent: the gated, accessible section-end prompt plus the compare step against the section's Samantekt in cleared chapters (D19). If [USER] wants interim recall as well, (c), so that one class's counters show its uptake before it spreads (S11.7 builds the prompt). If (a) stands, record the erratum.

**Decide before** S11.4, ruled at S10.2 after 4–6 weeks of counters. M5's prompt ships either way.

### 8.3 Call 3: the launch default (D-CALL3)

What do new and existing profiles get on deploy? As built, v1.1 silently switches every existing profile to paged [PED-01].

- (a) Scrolled for all; 'Síðuskipt' one tap away; stored only when picked. Cost: +1–2 sessions in S11.4.
- (b) Paged for all. Cost: +4–6 sessions (a notice naming 'Samfellt skrun', the figure rule, lazy images).
- (c) Paged for new profiles only, through S6.1's marker. Cost: as (b); realistically autumn 2027.
- (d) A rule keyed on viewport height or aspect, never width [PED-01]. Cost: not estimated.

**Evidence.** For paging: e-reader convention, and a trend (p = .066) towards better integrated understanding (R1-13, full text, university students). No difference: across studies, phones excluded (R1-04, full text), and on phones for adult crowdworkers (R1-14, full text). Against: in the same study as the trend, scrolling was more enjoyable on tablets (R1-13). The plan's '0.35–0.48 → 0.03–0.12' is an untested contrast, and the source's authors say their findings may be limited to college students (R1-05, full text). People tend to keep a default (d = 0.68, not reading-specific; R6-23, abstract). The costs of paging are in 4.6, 6.2 and 6.3 [PED-02, PED-03, CODE-09, UI-05].

**Measure.** The crossover (S10.1): half a class reads section A paged and B scrolled, half the reverse, with paper quizzes and a margin (how much worse still counts as "not worse") fixed in advance. A null means "no large harm", not equivalence.

**Recommendation for [USER]:** decide after the crossover, by a rule fixed before it: (b) or (c) only if paged is not worse on main-idea or detail items, or on calibration, within the margin, and fewer than half of students switch it off; otherwise (a). Until then nobody's mode changes. If a default is needed sooner: (a), with a line naming both modes.

**Decide before** S11.4, ruled at S10.2. A paged default before the crossover adds 4–6 sessions first.

### 8.4 Call 4: how often the pre-question appears (D-CALL4)

How often does the card appear? It is decided with P1.2's form (D16): read "real question" as "objective" if v1.2's form stays.

- (a) First visit to each unread section with a real question, remembered by module id. Cost: a record by module id.
- (b) v1.2's design: every visit until read. Cost: none.
- (c) First visit only, at most one card per calendar day or per study session ([USER] picks one; a session needs a definition without a backend, such as a browser-tab session); skipped sections stay eligible. Cost: as (a), plus a date or session check.
- (d) The first section per chapter; [USER] says whether that is the x.0 introduction (efni writes its question, H8) or the first numbered section. Cost: efni work for introductions.
- Any option may fade after 3 skips or 3 'Veit ekki' (proposed) in a row; deep-link arrival and a Settings choice (D17) belong here too.

**Evidence.** The benefit covers only the questioned content: teenagers g = 0.51 on asked items against 0.08 on the rest, and overall 1.25 with feedback against 0.60 without (R3-13, full text); 0.54 against 0.04 across studies (R2-09, abstract, mostly lab adults). For limits: repeated prompts lost their effect over a term (R5-05), pretests in an online course raised dropout (R2-08, full-text review), and response prompts limited low performers' transfer (R5-07, full text, German 6th graders).

**Measure.** No counters exist until P1.2 is live; then shown, answered, 'Veit ekki', covert and skipped counts on /greining.

**Recommendation for [USER]:** (c) for the first weeks, only where a real question with a key answer exists, one tap to skip, revisited after 4–6 weeks of counters. It is a pilot hedge: other sections read that day or session get no card, so they miss its benefit. vefur adds no page-type filter.

**Decide before** S13.1, with D16.

## 9. The way forward

Build the reader on main in small pieces, ordered by evidence and harm, and land the pager last, dark. "Dark" means on the live site but reachable only through special links, so no reader's mode changes until [USER] rules.

### 9.1 Three strategies

- **A. Integrate first.** Merge main into v1.1 now, while it costs one textual conflict and two semantic breaks; make v1.1 the one tree; ship v1.1.0 code-only over Christmas; then port P1 feature by feature.
- **B. Evidence-first redesign.** No intervention reaches students until it is in the strongest form the research supports: merge now, rebuild each section's end as question, recall and comparison with the Samantekt, turn Kvörðun into an action list, and decide paging by a classroom crossover. The cost is time.
- **C. Smallest shippable increments.** Every piece that stands alone goes to main and the live site as its own small PR, in order of evidence. No branch is merged; v1.1 is frozen as the porting source. The pager, which holds 54 of the 201 findings, the weakest evidence and three of the four open calls, comes last.

### 9.3 The recommended way and why

Recommendation for [USER]: strategy C, with ideas grafted from A and B (9.4). C scores highest, first for engineering and experience and four points behind B on learning. It fits one developer with about one vefur session a week after S (1 November 2026): every piece is a small PR, and each train (one deploy carrying the PRs merged since the last) can be reverted on its own.

Four things make it safe. The settings seam (a store that saves only what a reader chose, with a version and a profile-age marker) keeps every default reversible and every answer to call 3 possible. The deploy lane (a second checkout whose content mirrors the server) makes a code-only deploy really code-only. The pager lands last and dark: students reach it only after M9's pilot blockers are fixed. Print, second clicks on links, progress and the place kept across visits [CODE-08, CODE-10, UI-10, CODE-07] are fixed in M11, before any opt-in, so they are still unfixed in the crossover's link-only lessons; if D06 picks the early opt-in, those fixes move ahead of S9.6. ## 10. Step-by-step plan

The full plan is the companion file [2026-10-15-reader-v1.1-v1.2-review-plan.md](2026-10-15-reader-v1.1-v1.2-review-plan.md). There each step has its branch, files, 15-minute chunks with tick boxes, acceptance checks, prerequisites and estimate. This section lists every step and keeps what the rest of the report needs: how to use the plan, the capacity, each phase's goal and exit criteria, the handoffs to efni, what is deferred and the first session.

### How to use this plan

- **One step per session.** A step is one branch and one PR, or one deploy. Most take 0.5–1 session; the estimate is on each step.
- **Tick the chunks.** Each chunk is one action with a clear stopping point. Not all take 15 minutes: S0.1's two chunks do, but S9.3's three fill a whole session, so split a long chunk into 15-minute pieces when you start it. [USER]'s own reading, QA, lessons and question passes are marked as [USER]'s and can run longer. Tick a chunk when it is done, even if the step is not.
- **Ship at the end of each step.** Shipping means the step's PR is merged to main (in M0, the note or file is committed). Only the steps marked "Deploys: yes" put main on the live site. They are the trains: one deploy that carries every step merged since the last one. Every train runs `scripts/deploy.js` from the deploy lane (S0.5), and you read the dry run's deletion list before `--apply`.
- **When a step runs over,** stop at the end of a chunk. Push the branch (never main), write the next chunk at the top of the PR description or leave a `/snapshot`, and pick it up next session. If a step runs a whole session over, use the cut order below. If the same error comes back three times, step back and try another approach.
- **Decisions come first.** "Decide first" names the decision that must be on file in docs/decisions/ before the step starts. Section 8 has the options and recommendations. The decision sessions are S0.2, S9.0, S12.0 and the ruling chunk of S10.2. A step whose decision is missing waits; the next step that needs none goes ahead.
- **Trains wait for decisions too.** A train carries everything merged since the last one, so no train may run while main holds a merged feature whose own train step is waiting on a decision: hold the train, or keep that feature switched off on main until its own train.
- **Port whole files.** Where a step copies code from v1.1 or v1.2, copy whole files at the named commit and cite the commit's SHA (its short id) in the PR. Hand-wiring a page from memory can silently drop one of the rulings, so the 19 pagination tests run after every pager step.

**Capacity.** Supply is about 5.5 sessions before S (1 November 2026), then about one a week, none in a mid-December content-release week, and about 4 over the break. Demand to the spring term (M0–M4) is about 16.5 sessions, with the rating-scale fix in S4.2, so predict-first reaches the term boundary only if S3.3 moves to M5's train and S4.3, the first cut, waits. Demand to a crossover-ready pager (M9) is about 37 sessions: late May 2027 at one session a week, late March at 1.5. By the end of May that is about nine trains at one session a week and up to eleven at 1.5; each costs half a session or more, already counted in the step estimates. Each phase gives both dates.

**Cut order** when a phase runs long: its stretch items first (the compare step, then the counts export), then the PredictThenRate extraction (S4.3), then the filler queue's SF.3–SF.5. Never cut an accessibility blocker (SF.1 and SF.2 among them) or the settings seam.

**Words used in this plan.**

- **Dark:** on the live site, but reachable only through a special link, so no reader's mode changes.
- **Deploy lane:** a second clone of main used only for deploys, whose content matches the server's file for file.
- **Settings seam:** a settings store that saves only what a reader chose, with a version number and a marker of when the profile was created, so a default can change later.
- **readerPolicy.ts:** one small file that holds every rule an open call decides, so a ruling changes one value.
- **Decision session:** a session in which [USER] rules from written briefs; each ruling, or each deferral to a named later session, goes into its own dated, append-only file in docs/decisions/.
- **Announcer:** one hidden region that screen readers read aloud when something changes.
- **e2e:** end-to-end tests that drive a real browser (Playwright).
- **axe:** an automated accessibility checker. A **ratchet** records today's failures and fails on any new one.
- **Mutant:** a bug planted on purpose to see whether the tests catch it.
- **Cherry-pick:** copy one commit onto another branch. **Port:** bring a feature to main this way, or by copying whole files.
- **Fit-to-offer set:** the fixes paged mode needs before anyone may switch it on: print, second clicks on links, progress that follows the pager, the place kept across visits, and an accessible 'Lestrarstilling'.
- **Crossover:** a classroom comparison in which each student reads one section paged and one scrolled, in counterbalanced order.
- **② preview:** efni's whole-book re-inject preview, which is this machine's local content. It is not the live content, and numbers measured on it say "preview content".

## Appendix B. Citation audit and sources

This appendix has two parts. B.1 lists every research claim in the reader plan or the branch code that the six research briefs (R1–R6) checked, with a verdict and a correction. B.2 lists the sources the report relies on, by research area. What the audit means for the plan is in [PED-26].

Four facts apply to everything below.

- **No study tests 16–20-year-olds reading chemistry, or any STEM text, on phones.** The evidence comes from university students, adults or younger pupils. The closest phone-sized study used 27 high-school students with dyslexia reading 208-word passages on a 2013 handheld (Schneps et al. 2013, R1-20). R1 found no experiment that compares paged with scrolled reading of long expository or science sections in 15–20-year-olds, on phones or any other device.
- **The plan's own evidence base could not be checked.** It is a Claude-generated literature review from April 2026, and it is not in the repository [PED-26].
- **Each source has a verification level.** Full text means the reviewer read the paper or an author manuscript. Abstract means only the abstract or a database record. Secondary means another source reported it. Much of the evidence was read only as abstracts, so its numbers carry more doubt.
- **How to read the numbers.** g and d are standardised effect sizes (about 0.2 is small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large). k is the number of studies or effect sizes pooled. n.s. means not statistically significant.

### C.2 Contrast ratios
